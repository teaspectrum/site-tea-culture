What it is

Record of Yangxian Teapots (Yangxian Minghu Xi) is a late-Ming monograph on Yixing zisha teapots by Zhou Gaoqi, completed around 1640, the thirteenth year of the Chongzhen reign. It is one volume of about 3,500 characters. Yangxian is the ancient name of Yixing; the title means a genealogy of famous Yangxian pots. It is not a general ceramics book but a ranked record of makers and wares.

What it contains

After a preface, the book classifies makers into seven categories: founders, proper beginnings, great masters, famous makers, elegant followers, extraordinary works and other schools. It records thirty-one pot makers, including Gongchun and Shi Dabin, and discusses clay sources, workshop locations, firing and the suitability of pots for tea. The preface's claim that local clay “brings out the true colour and fragrance of tea”, and that a fine pot could cost ten or twenty taels of silver, is often quoted because it links practical function with late-Ming market value.

Why it matters

It is one of the earliest systematic treatments of Yixing zisha and provided a model for later works such as the Record of Yangxian Famous Ceramics and the Illustrated Record of Tea Pots. For modern readers its value is not a buying guide but a view of how late-Ming writers judged a pot: clay, form, workmanship, maker lineage and performance in use. Zhou connects craft with function, which is more useful than a simple ranking of famous names.

Common misunderstandings

First, “earliest” does not mean “entirely reliable”. The Gongchun and Jinsha Temple stories contain legend, and the book itself says some information was heard from pot makers. Second, it is not a modern authentication manual: it has no clay composition, firing temperature or microscopic data. Third, the price of “ten or twenty taels” belongs to a specific late-Ming market and cannot be converted directly into today's prices.

How to read it

Read the preface first for the core judgement that local clay brings out tea's colour and aroma; then the founder and “proper beginnings” chapters for the early lineage; finally the masters, famous makers, elegant followers, extraordinary works and other schools for different criteria. Editions include the Tanji congshu and Cuilanggan guan versions, with modern facsimile and translated editions. Cite the edition, and do not treat a modern translation as the original text.

Editions

Modern readers usually use a facsimile or an annotated translation. Check whether the editor has kept the seven maker categories and the original preface, and whether the illustrations are from the same edition. A modern “complete translation” may add commentary that is not in the Ming text; keep the two layers separate when quoting.

Source boundary

This page relies on the Yixing Municipal Archives and a facsimile/translation of ancient zisha texts. Zhou's birth and death dates are given variously as 1596–1645; the date of the book is around 1640. This page uses the broad “late Ming, around 1640” frame rather than a single certain year.
