Song whisked tea and tea contests

History

宋代点茶与斗茶是末茶饮法的精致化：饼茶炙碾罗末，盏预热，调膏、注汤、击拂，以汤色纯白、汤花细密、水痕慢退为胜。建窑黑釉盏、茶筅、茶匙、汤瓶构成器物体系；斗茶是文人、茶人和富户的技艺竞赛。

Song whisked tea and tea contests refined powdered-tea drinking: cake tea was roasted, ground and sieved; the bowl was warmed; paste was made, water added and whisked; pure white colour, fine even foam and a slow-retreating water mark won. Jian black-glaze bowls, chasen, spoon and water bottle formed the vessel system. Contests were a craft competition among literati, tea specialists and wealthy households. It is not a synonym for Japanese matcha, nor a “foam-making performance.”

The whisking sequence

First prepare the tea: roast the cake soft, grind to powder, sieve fine; finer powder gives more even foam. Second prepare the vessels: warm the bowl (xiezhan), rinse the whisk/spoon, heat water in the bottle. Third whisk: put powder in, add a little boiling water to make paste, then add water in stages and whisk fast until powder and water combine and white foam forms.

Cai Xiang's Record of Tea divides the flow into storage, roasting, grinding, sieving, water, warming the bowl and whisking; Emperor Huizong's Treatise on Tea adds “seven pours,” each with different water volume, whisking force and surface state. Whisking is not “stir water”; it is a continuous control of powder-water ratio, temperature and speed.

How contests were judged

Contests compared colour, foam and water mark. Pure white was best; blue-white and yellow-white next. Foam should be fine, even and “bite the bowl” (cling to the wall); the water mark should recede slowly, and the first to expose the line lost. Fine powder, good water, accurate fire and fast whisking could win. Contests also judged vessels, tea and bearing, not only taste.

“Biting the bowl” is not mysticism: tea saponins, proteins and fine particles stabilise foam; whisking entrains air, and finer, stronger tea holds it longer. But more foam does not mean better taste; contest criteria are visual and technical, while daily drinking still judges aroma and flavour.

Vessel system

The Jian hare's-fur bowl was the standard: dark glaze set off white foam, thick body held heat, a slightly curved wall helped whisking. Cai Xiang says “tea is white, so black bowls suit it”; Jian'an ware is “dark purple-black, patterned like hare's fur,” while others are “thin or purple, all inferior.” The chasen (bamboo whisk) beats, the spoon makes paste, the water bottle controls the pour, the grinder and sieve control powder.

Vessels are not “the more expensive the better”: the bowl must be hot, the whisk resilient, the bottle steady, the grinder even. Jian “hare's fur,” “oil spot” and “yohen” are glaze effects, not taste indicators; treating a Jian bowl as a guarantee of “top Song tea” ignores the leaf.

Whisking and “tea painting”

Beyond whisking, there was “fencha” or “tea hundred games”: drawing patterns and characters on the foam with a spoon or water stream, a kind of tea latte art. It needed very fine powder, stable foam and skill; literati treated it as a elegant game, not a daily necessity. It appears in Song texts and poems, but the exact technique is lost and modern reconstructions are speculative.

Whisked tea also served monastery offerings, court gifts, wedding rites and literati gatherings. Tea was not only a drink but ritual, game and social glue. Reducing it to “tea contests” ignores its religious, ceremonial and everyday dimensions.

Brewing and boundaries

To try it today, use matcha: 1–2 g to 60–80 ml water at 70–80°C, make paste first, then whisk in a W motion for 30–60 seconds; warm the bowl and rinse the whisk. For a closer Song feel, use finer powder and faster whisking, but cultivar and steaming differ, so it is not a full reconstruction.

Song whisked tea is not “Japanese chanoyu”; Japanese matcha has its own room, etiquette and schools. Contests were not “a national sport”; they belonged mainly to literati, tea specialists and wealthy households. Jian bowls are whisking vessels, not universal Song cups; the Song also drank loose and boiled tea.

Japanese tea ceremony

日本茶道（chanoyu）是以抹茶为核心、在茶室中待客的综合艺术，千利休确立侘寂与“和敬清寂”。它包含茶室、挂轴、花、炭、怀石、浓茶/薄茶和主客礼仪；表千家、里千家、武者小路千家等流派各有程序，不是“打一碗抹茶”的表演。
